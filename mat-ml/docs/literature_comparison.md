targets, units and above all the **train/test protocol** differ. The "comparable?" column says why. Nothing below should be read as "we beat" or "we lose to" a published number.

## What can be said fairly
- On the log scale our out-of-fold stiffness error (ln MAE 0.28 for random and chemical-system splits, 0.39 for unseen families) is of the same order as the in-database error reported for a graph network (0.18-0.19) whose test set included training materials; the difference is not interpretable without a like-for-like protocol.
- None of the papers evaluated on held-out chemical systems or structure families; all used random, in-domain or same-material test sets. The family-grouped and monolayer-grouped results here are therefore not directly comparable to any of them, and the gap between our random and grouped numbers (1.4x for stiffness, 3.7-5.6x for binding energy) is the quantity those protocols would be expected to hide.
- A like-for-like comparison would require retraining a graph model (CGCNN) on the same 7,258 materials with the same grouped folds. That was not done.
